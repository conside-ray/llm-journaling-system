## Insights

### Clear task structure supports momentum when mood is uneven
- Proposed destination: `promotion/insights.md`
- Candidate: A clear framework for defining tasks appears to improve productive momentum even when energy and mood are uneven.
- Why it matters: This suggests that improving task structure may be a practical way to preserve execution without waiting for a better internal state.
- Evidence: On June 3, a better task framework coincided with feeling “much more productive.” Across the week, all listed tasks were completed while moods ranged from uneven and wavering to blocked.
- Confidence: Medium. The relationship appeared clearly this week but needs repetition across additional weeks.
- Status: Pending review

### Task completion can conceal an unresolved bottleneck
- Proposed destination: `promotion/insights.md`
- Candidate: Completing assigned tasks does not necessarily mean the highest-leverage constraint is being addressed; a sense of blockage after a fully completed day is a signal to inspect outcomes and bottlenecks.
- Why it matters: Without this distinction, the system may reward closed tasks while missing weak conversion, unclear direction, or workflow constraints.
- Evidence: On June 5, all assigned tasks were completed, but the journal still reported a clear feeling that something was preventing greater progress. Several actions during the week were recorded as complete without their external outcomes.
- Confidence: Medium. The contrast is concrete, but the underlying bottleneck has not yet been identified.
- Status: Pending review
